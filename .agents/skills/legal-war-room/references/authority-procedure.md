# Authority and Procedure Reference

## Authority verification

For each authority relied upon, record:
- full citation and court;
- decision/statute/rule date;
- current status;
- jurisdiction and precedential level;
- exact proposition supported;
- pinpoint;
- adverse treatment or amendment;
- materially distinguishing facts.

Prefer official court and legislative sources for operative law. Use reputable secondary sources to discover authority, not as a substitute for controlling primary law.

## Procedural vehicle checklist

Before selecting a vehicle, answer:
1. What exact relief is sought?
2. What source gives the tribunal power to grant it?
3. What ordinary mechanism exists?
4. Is extraordinary relief necessary?
5. What standard governs?
6. What factual record is required?
7. Who must be joined or served?
8. What deadline applies?
9. What filing fee, waiver, RJI, notice, return-date or e-filing rule applies?
10. Is the resulting order appealable, enforceable and sufficiently definite?

## Government-duty analysis

For mandamus or government-delay matters, isolate the statutory sentence imposing the duty. Identify the actor, verb, object, deadline and conditions. Determine whether any judgment or discretion remains. Separate compelling an official to act from compelling a particular discretionary outcome.

## Service verification

Verify recipient identity and legal capacity independently from the street address. A correct building with the wrong legal recipient is not sufficient. Confirm current office/floor details from an authoritative court or government source whenever available.

## Deadline verification

Record:
- triggering event;
- governing rule;
- base period;
- method-of-service adjustment;
- weekend/holiday rule;
- computed date;
- source used.

Never calculate from an assumed service date.
